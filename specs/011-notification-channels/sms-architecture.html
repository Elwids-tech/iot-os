How SMS alerts would work

A draft for spec 011. SMS is a second personal channel next to email: it uses the same alert path, recipients and retry rules. It adds a verified mobile number, per-person SMS levels and a monthly SMS allowance from each organization's plan.

Draft · 6 November 2026 · One Twilio toll-free number · US and Canadian numbers · Nothing here is built yet

1. Where SMS fits

An alert reaches the API the way it does today. In the same database transaction, the alert projector creates one delivery per recipient and channel, and one per Teams or Slack destination. Each delivery is a queued job. SMS adds a new job beside the email job; nothing upstream changes.

Exists todayNew in spec 011Outside service

Why a job per channel: email and SMS succeed or fail on their own. A Twilio outage retries only the SMS deliveries, and an email bounce never blocks a text. It is the same pattern as DeliverAlertEmail today: the row is created in the alert's transaction, so a crash between "alert recorded" and "message queued" cannot lose a message.

2. One SMS, step by step

What the new DeliverAlertSms job does for one recipient. Steps 2 to 4 run under a row lock on the delivery, as the email job does.

  1. Delivery created

    Only for recipients the workflow sends SMS to. Status pending, queued on alerts in the alert's transaction.

  2. Checks, repeated before every attempt

    Everything email checks today (still a member with device view, still on the subscription, workflow not stopped, not already cleared), plus: a verified number, not opted out, the country allowed, and the person's SMS levels include this severity. A failed check cancels the delivery with a reason.

  3. Write the text (two parts at most)

    Long names are shortened so the message never needs more than two parts.

  4. Count it and reserve the allowance

    Each part counts as one SMS. Lock the organization's allowance row for this month: if what is left covers the parts, add them to used; otherwise cancel with "SMS allowance used up for this month".

  5. Send through Twilio

    From the deployment's toll-free number. Twilio's answer decides the outcome (table below).

Sender new

One toll-free number for the deployment, verified with Twilio before it can send. It reaches US and Canadian mobile numbers, which are the only ones accepted for now. Other countries need their own sender and cost up to 25 times as much, so they are later work.

Allowance new

The number of SMS the organization's plan includes each month (UTC). One row per organization per month: allowance and used. The row lock makes concurrent sends wait their turn, so a burst can't go over. Until plans exist in the platform, admins enter each organization's allowance; 0 means no SMS.

A part is 160 characters, or 70 with non-Latin text. Most alerts fit in one; none take more than two. Each part costs us about $0.012–0.017 with carrier fees, so 500 SMS costs at most about $8.50.

Verification codes

Our own six-digit codes, sent from the same toll-free number through the Messages API rather than Twilio Verify, which charges for each verification on top. They belong to the person, so they don't use any organization's allowance. 5 wrong tries per code, 5 codes per person per hour, and a deployment-wide hourly cap.

3. Getting numbers from existing members

Nobody has a phone number today, and SMS does nothing until people add one. There are three ways in. The main one is a banner after sign-in; managers can also nudge the recipients of a workflow.

Who sees the banner

Only when all of these hold, so nobody is nagged about something that can't work:

  • The deployment has Twilio set up.
  • The organization's SMS allowance is above 0.
  • The person can view devices in this organization.
  • They have no verified number, or it's opted out.
  • They haven't chosen "Not now" in the last 14 days. After "Don't ask me again", it returns only when a manager adds them to another SMS workflow or asks them for a number.

Why a banner, not a pop-up

  • People often sign in from an alert email to act on it. A pop-up gets in the way at the worst moment.
  • Verifying needs the phone in hand. A pop-up they can't finish now is closed and forgotten; a banner waits.
  • It reuses the portal's existing banner slot, with no new layer over the page.

B · Manager's nudge

On a workflow's settings, recipients without a number show No number. Ask them to add one emails each one a link to the same dialog and brings their banner back, even after "Don't ask me again". At most once per person a week.

C · Preferences

Any time, under Settings → Notification preferences (today's Email preferences, renamed). Change or remove the number there too.

D · New members (later)

An optional "mobile number for alerts" step when someone accepts an invitation. Skippable; the banner covers anyone who skips it.

A manager can't type in someone else's number. Only the owner can add it, because it must be verified with a code sent to that phone. That keeps texts from going to a wrong or unwilling number and is what carriers expect for consent.

Count parts or messages?

The spec counts each 160-character part as one SMS, which matches what Twilio charges. Counting whole messages is simpler to explain on a pricing page but doubles our cost for a two-part alert.

Plans in the platform

There are no plans in the code yet, so admins type in each organization's allowance. Plans with their alert and SMS limits need a spec of their own.

Banner timings

14 days for "Not now", and at most one manager request per person a week. Both are defaults to confirm.

Other countries, later

They need a local sender (often a registered alphanumeric ID, weeks per country), and an SMS that costs far more than in North America would have to count as several.
